## The idea

*Fent poble* ("making community") starts from the idea that a community forms where all its inhabitants meet. La Soledad grew around the Can Ribes factory and its workers' houses, where life happened in the street and daily routines were shared. The project sets out to regenerate those dynamics and give back an identity to a neighbourhood that has lost it, building on its living memory.

## Common ground

The roofs become shared spaces that encourage community life and host everyday tasks such as drying laundry. West-facing terraces at the back act as circulation and carry planting for shade, privacy and rest. Spaces usually left to services and maintenance, such as the roof and a ground-floor passage with bicycle parking, are turned into places for neighbours to meet.

## Learning from the neighbourhood

The building is meant to add value to a consolidated urban fabric rather than stand out from it. Answers to design problems come from the neighbourhood itself: its courtyards, streets, planting and materials, and the way people use them. Comfort relies on traditional, everyday means (shade, cross ventilation, vegetation as a climatic filter, ceramic construction) instead of short-lived technical fixes, suited to Mallorca's humidity and the sea breeze of the *embat*.

## The dwellings

Homes are ordered by sun and prevailing winds. The living core opens to the south to collect winter heat and summer breezes, and day rooms run through the depth of the unit for cross ventilation. Kitchens and bathrooms are grouped in a compact central core, leaving the remaining rooms flexible. The scheme includes one, two and three-bedroom units, through-courtyards and a Mallorcan-style courtyard inside the block.

## Climate strategy

- Rainwater collection
- Centralised pellet boiler
- Cross ventilation with the *embat* breeze
- Ventilated west façade
- Pitched roof that lets warm air stratify and dissipate on the top floor
